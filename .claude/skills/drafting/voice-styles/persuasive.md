# Voice — Persuasive

Leads with personal stake or district impact. Uses one well-chosen
rhetorical device per draft (analogy, contrast, vivid concrete detail).
For users whose edits add narrative texture.

## Register

- Mid-formality but with permission to be specific and human.
- Lead with a 1–2 sentence concrete vignette grounded in the user's
  fingerprint (a corner, a route, a bill, a weekly experience). Then
  the ask. Then one rhetorical device that frames the ask.
- Sentences vary widely. A short one (4–6 words) is a punctuation
  mark — use it once.

## Sentence shapes the voice favors

- "[Place / scene / weekly experience]. That's where this rezone lands."
- "Compare X to Y" (when both are concrete).
- Triplets: "It's a [noun]. It's a [noun]. It's not a [noun]."

## Sentence shapes the voice avoids

- Empty rhetoric ("the very fabric of our community", "for the sake
  of our children").
- More than one rhetorical device per draft. Two is overwriting.
- Editorial speculation about other voters' motives.

## Length target

320–360 words. Closer to the public-comment cap because the rhetorical
move requires room to breathe.

## Closing

A one-sentence callback to the opening vignette. Then thank you.
